🧹 週末の掃除の日 (Weekend Cleaning Day)

土曜日の朝、山本家では大掃除をすることになりました。お母さんが「みんな、今日は家族で掃除をしますよ」と言いました。子供たちは少し嫌そうな顔をしましたが、協力することにしました。

Doyoubi no asa, Yamamoto-ke de wa oosouji wo suru koto ni narimashita. Okaasan ga "Minna, kyou wa kazoku de souji wo shimasu yo" to iimashita. Kodomo-tachi wa sukoshi iya-sou na kao wo shimashita ga, kyouryoku suru koto ni shimashita. • On Saturday morning, the Yamamoto family decided to do a big cleaning. Mother said, "Everyone, today we're cleaning as a family." The children made slightly reluctant faces, but decided to cooperate.
🎌 Cultural Note: 大掃除 (oosouji - "big cleaning") is a major tradition in Japan, especially before New Year's. However, many families also do thorough cleaning on weekends. It's considered a family activity where everyone participates, teaching children responsibility and cooperation.

お父さんは掃除機で居間と廊下を掃除しました。掃除機の音が家中に響きました。その後、床を拭いて、窓ガラスも磨きました。窓がピカピカになって、お父さんは満足そうでした。

Otousan wa soujiki de ima to rouka wo souji shimashita. Soujiki no oto ga ienaka ni hibikimashita. Sono ato, yuka wo fuite, mado garasu mo migakimashita. Mado ga pikapika ni natte, otousan wa manzoku-sou deshita. • Father vacuumed the living room and hallway. The sound of the vacuum cleaner echoed throughout the house. After that, he wiped the floor and polished the windows too. The windows became sparkling clean, and father looked satisfied.

お母さんは台所の掃除を担当しました。シンクを磨いて、コンロの油汚れを落としました。冷蔵庫の中も整理して、賞味期限が切れた食品を捨てました。台所がとても清潔になりました。

Okaasan wa daidokoro no souji wo tantou shimashita. Shinku wo migaite, konro no abura-yogore wo otoshimashita. Reizouko no naka mo seiri shite, shoumikigen ga kireta shokuhin wo sutemashita. Daidokoro ga totemo seiketsu ni narimashita. • Mother was in charge of cleaning the kitchen. She polished the sink and removed oil stains from the stove. She also organized the inside of the refrigerator and threw away expired food. The kitchen became very clean.
📝 Grammar Tip: ～を担当する (wo tantou suru) means "to be in charge of." It's commonly used when dividing tasks or responsibilities: 掃除を担当する (souji wo tantou suru - "be in charge of cleaning").

長女の美咲は自分の部屋を片付けました。散らかっていた服をクローゼットに入れて、本棚の本を整理しました。机の上の書類も整頓して、ゴミを集めてゴミ箱に捨てました。

Choujo no Misaki wa jibun no heya wo katazukemashita. Chirakatte ita fuku wo kuroozetto ni irete, hondana no hon wo seiri shimashita. Tsukue no ue no shorui mo seiton shite, gomi wo atsumete gomibako ni sutemashita. • The eldest daughter Misaki tidied up her own room. She put scattered clothes in the closet and organized books on the bookshelf. She also arranged documents on her desk and collected trash to throw in the garbage can.

長男の大輔は庭の掃除をしました。落ち葉を集めて袋に入れました。雑草も抜いて、庭がすっきりしました。最後に花壇に水をやって、庭仕事が終わりました。

Chounan no Daisuke wa niwa no souji wo shimashita. Ochiba wo atsumete fukuro ni iremashita. Zassou mo nuite, niwa ga sukkiri shimashita. Saigo ni kadan ni mizu wo yatte, niwa-shigoto ga owarimashita. • The eldest son Daisuke cleaned the garden. He collected fallen leaves and put them in bags. He also pulled weeds, and the garden became neat. Finally, he watered the flower bed, and the garden work was finished.
📝 Grammar Tip: すっきりする (sukkiri suru) describes the feeling or state of being refreshed, neat, or uncluttered. It's often used after cleaning or organizing: 部屋がすっきりした (heya ga sukkiri shita - "the room became neat and tidy").

午後三時に全ての掃除が終わりました。家族全員が疲れていましたが、きれいになった家を見て嬉しくなりました。お母さんが「みんなありがとう。今日はよく頑張りましたね」と言いました。夕方、家族でケーキを食べて、掃除の疲れを癒しました。

Gogo san-ji ni subete no souji ga owarimashita. Kazoku zen'in ga tsukarete imashita ga, kirei ni natta ie wo mite ureshiku narimashita. Okaasan ga "Minna arigatou. Kyou wa yoku ganbarimashita ne" to iimashita. Yuugata, kazoku de keeki wo tabete, souji no tsukare wo iyashimashita. • At 3 PM all the cleaning was finished. The whole family was tired, but felt happy seeing the clean house. Mother said, "Thank you everyone. You all worked hard today." In the evening, the family ate cake together and healed their cleaning fatigue.
🎌 Cultural Note: After completing household tasks together, Japanese families often reward themselves with treats or special meals. This reinforces the idea that working together as a family is valuable and worth celebrating, strengthening family bonds.

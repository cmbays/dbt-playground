🌙 家でのリラックスタイム (Relaxing Evening at Home)

午後六時、鈴木家の家族が仕事や学校から帰ってきました。玄関で「ただいま」と言って、靴を脱ぎました。お母さんが「お帰りなさい。夕ご飯の準備ができていますよ」と笑顔で迎えました。

Gogo roku-ji, Suzuki-ke no kazoku ga shigoto ya gakkou kara kaette kimashita. Genkan de "Tadaima" to itte, kutsu wo nugimashita. Okaasan ga "Okaerinasai. Yuugohan no junbi ga dekite imasu yo" to egao de mukaemashita. • At 6 PM, the Suzuki family returned from work and school. They said "Tadaima" at the entrance and took off their shoes. Mother greeted them with a smile saying, "Welcome home. Dinner is ready."
🎌 Cultural Note: "ただいま" (tadaima - "I'm home") and "お帰りなさい" (okaerinasai - "welcome home") are essential daily greetings in Japanese homes. These phrases create a sense of belonging and reinforce that home is a safe, welcoming space for family members.

家族全員が食卓に集まって、夕食を食べました。今日のメニューはカレーライスとサラダでした。お父さんは会社での出来事を話し、子供たちは学校での楽しい話をしました。笑い声が食卓に響きました。

Kazoku zen'in ga shokutaku ni atsumatte, yuushoku wo tabemashita. Kyou no menyuu wa karee raisu to sarada deshita. Otousan wa kaisha de no dekigoto wo hanashi, kodomo-tachi wa gakkou de no tanoshii hanashi wo shimashita. Warai-goe ga shokutaku ni hibikimashita. • The whole family gathered at the dining table and ate dinner. Today's menu was curry rice and salad. Father talked about events at the company, and the children shared fun stories from school. Laughter echoed around the dining table.

夕食の後、お母さんと娘が一緒に食器を洗いました。お父さんと息子はテーブルを片付けて、居間の掃除をしました。家事を分担することで、早く終わりました。「協力してくれてありがとう」とお母さんが言いました。

Yuushoku no ato, okaasan to musume ga issho ni shokki wo araimashita. Otousan to musuko wa teeburu wo katazukete, ima no souji wo shimashita. Kaji wo buntan suru koto de, hayaku owarimashita. "Kyouryoku shite kurete arigatou" to okaasan ga iimashita. • After dinner, mother and daughter washed dishes together. Father and son tidied the table and cleaned the living room. By sharing the housework, they finished quickly. Mother said, "Thank you for cooperating."
📝 Grammar Tip: ～することで (suru koto de) means "by doing [something]" and expresses method or means: 分担することで (buntan suru koto de - "by sharing/dividing"). It shows how an action leads to a result.

八時になると、子供たちはお風呂に入りました。まず娘が入って、次に息子が入りました。お風呂から出ると、パジャマに着替えました。髪を乾かして、歯を磨きました。お風呂の後は体が温まって、気持ちが良かったです。

Hachi-ji ni naru to, kodomo-tachi wa ofuro ni hairimashita. Mazu musume ga haitte, tsugi ni musuko ga hairimashita. Ofuro kara deru to, pajama ni kigaemashita. Kami wo kawakashite, ha wo migakimashita. Ofuro no ato wa karada ga atatamatte, kimochi ga yokatta desu. • When it became 8 o'clock, the children took baths. First the daughter went in, then the son went in. When they came out of the bath, they changed into pajamas. They dried their hair and brushed their teeth. After the bath their bodies were warm and they felt good.
🎌 Cultural Note: In Japanese homes, お風呂 (ofuro - bath) is an important nightly ritual for relaxation, not just cleaning. Family members typically bathe one after another in the same hot water. The bath is taken after washing the body outside the tub, keeping the bathwater clean for everyone.

九時から家族全員が居間に集まって、一緒にテレビを見ました。今日は家族で楽しめるクイズ番組を見ました。みんなで答えを考えて、正解すると喜びました。このような時間が家族の絆を強くします。

Ku-ji kara kazoku zen'in ga ima ni atsumatte, issho ni terebi wo mimashita. Kyou wa kazoku de tanoshimeru kuizu bangumi wo mimashita. Minna de kotae wo kangaete, seikai suru to yorokobimashita. Kono you na jikan ga kazoku no kizuna wo tsuyoku shimasu. • From 9 o'clock the whole family gathered in the living room and watched TV together. Today they watched a quiz show that the family could enjoy. Everyone thought about the answers, and rejoiced when they got them right. This kind of time strengthens family bonds.

十時になると、子供たちは自分の部屋に戻りました。明日の学校の準備をして、ベッドに入りました。お母さんが部屋に来て、「おやすみなさい」と言いました。お父さんとお母さんは少しリビングでお茶を飲んでから、寝ました。静かな夜が鈴木家を包みました。

Juu-ji ni naru to, kodomo-tachi wa jibun no heya ni modorimashita. Ashita no gakkou no junbi wo shite, beddo ni hairimashita. Okaasan ga heya ni kite, "Oyasuminasai" to iimashita. Otousan to okaasan wa sukoshi ribingu de ocha wo nonde kara, nemashita. Shizuka na yoru ga Suzuki-ke wo tsutsumimashita. • When it became 10 o'clock, the children returned to their own rooms. They prepared for school tomorrow and got into bed. Mother came to their rooms and said "Oyasuminasai." Father and mother drank tea in the living room for a bit, then went to sleep. A quiet night enveloped the Suzuki household.
📝 Grammar Tip: ～てから (te kara) means "after doing": 飲んでから (nonde kara - "after drinking"). It shows the sequence of actions clearly, indicating that one action is completed before the next begins.

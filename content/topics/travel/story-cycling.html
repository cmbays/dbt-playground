🚲 郊外サイクリング (Suburban Cycling)

週末、中村さんは自転車で郊外を探索することにしました。天気が良く、サイクリングに最適な日でした。朝九時に自宅を出発し、のどかな田園風景を目指して漕ぎ始めました。

Shuumatsu, Nakamura-san wa jitensha de kougai wo tansaku suru koto ni shimashita. Tenki ga yoku, saikuringu ni saiteki na hi deshita. Asa ku-ji ni jitaku wo shuppatsu shi, nodoka na den'en fuukei wo mezashite kogi hajimemashita. • On the weekend, Mr. Nakamura decided to explore the suburbs by bicycle. The weather was good, and it was a perfect day for cycling. He left home at 9 AM and started pedaling toward the peaceful rural scenery.
🎌 Cultural Note: Cycling (サイクリング saikuringu or 自転車旅行 jitensha ryokou) is increasingly popular in Japan for both recreation and eco-friendly transportation. Many rural areas have dedicated cycling routes (サイクリングロード saikuringu roodo) with beautiful scenery. Cyclists can rent bicycles at train stations in tourist areas.

住宅街を抜けると、道は静かな田舎道になりました。両側には田んぼと畑が広がり、遠くには山々が見えました。新鮮な空気を吸いながら、ゆっくりとペダルを漕ぎました。

Juutaku-gai wo nukeru to, michi wa shizuka na inaka-michi ni narimashita. Ryougawa ni wa tanbo to hatake ga hirogari, tooku ni wa yama-yama ga miemashita. Shinsen na kuuki wo sui nagara, yukkuri to pedaru wo kogimashita. • After passing through the residential area, the road became a quiet country road. Rice paddies and fields spread out on both sides, and mountains could be seen in the distance. He slowly pedaled while breathing in the fresh air.
📝 Grammar Tip: ～を抜ける (wo nukeru) means "to pass through" or "to get through": 住宅街を抜ける (juutaku-gai wo nukeru - "pass through the residential area"), トンネルを抜ける (tonneru wo nukeru - "go through the tunnel").

小さな村を通り過ぎると、古い神社を見つけました。中村さんは自転車を停めて、境内を散歩しました。静かで平和な雰囲気に心が落ち着きました。地元の人が親切に挨拶してくれました。

Chiisa na mura wo toori-sugiru to, furui jinja wo mitsukemashita. Nakamura-san wa jitensha wo tomete, keidai wo sanpo shimashita. Shizuka de heiwa na fun'iki ni kokoro ga ochitsuki mashita. Jimoto no hito ga shinsetsu ni aisatsu shite kuremashita. • When he passed through a small village, he found an old shrine. Mr. Nakamura stopped his bicycle and walked around the grounds. His heart calmed down in the quiet and peaceful atmosphere. A local person kindly greeted him.

午後、小川のほとりで休憩しました。持ってきたお弁当を食べ、川のせせらぎを聞きながらリラックスしました。近くで釣りをしている人や、散歩している家族を見かけました。

Gogo, ogawa no hotori de kyuukei shimashita. Motte kita obentou wo tabe, kawa no seseragi wo kiki nagara rirakkusu shimashita. Chikaku de tsuri wo shite iru hito ya, sanpo shite iru kazoku wo mikakemashita. • In the afternoon, he took a break by a small stream. He ate the lunch box he brought and relaxed while listening to the murmuring of the river. He saw people fishing nearby and families taking walks.
🎌 Cultural Note: お弁当 (obentou - boxed lunch) is an integral part of Japanese culture. People often prepare or buy beautifully arranged bento boxes for outings, picnics, and day trips. Convenience stores sell affordable ready-made bento that are popular for excursions.

帰り道、農産物直売所に立ち寄りました。新鮮な野菜と果物、それから手作りのジャムを買いました。店の人と少し会話して、地域の情報を教えてもらいました。

Kaeri-michi, nousanbutsu chokubaijo ni tachiyori mashita. Shinsen na yasai to kudamono, sorekara tezukuri no jamu wo kaimashita. Mise no hito to sukoshi kaiwa shite, chiiki no jouhou wo oshiete moraimashita. • On the way back, he stopped by a farm produce stand. He bought fresh vegetables and fruits, as well as handmade jam. He chatted a bit with the shop person and was told about local information.
📝 Grammar Tip: ～てもらう (te morau) means "to receive/get someone to do something for you": 教えてもらいました (oshiete moraimashita - "I was taught/told"). It's similar to ～てくれる but emphasizes the receiver's benefit more.

夕方、中村さんは自宅に戻りました。約四十キロメートルを走り、足は疲れていましたが、心は満たされていました。自然の中で過ごした一日は、ストレスを忘れさせてくれました。また来週もサイクリングに出かけたいと思いました。

Yuugata, Nakamura-san wa jitaku ni modorimashita. Yaku yonjuu kiromeetoru wo hashiri, ashi wa tsukarete imashita ga, kokoro wa mitasarete imashita. Shizen no naka de sugoshita ichinichi wa, sutoresu wo wasuresasete kuremashita. Mata raishuu mo saikuringu ni dekake tai to omoimashita. • In the evening, Mr. Nakamura returned home. He rode about 40 kilometers, and his legs were tired, but his heart was fulfilled. The day spent in nature made him forget his stress. He thought he'd like to go cycling again next week.
📝 Grammar Tip: ～させる (saseru) is the causative form. When combined with ～てくれる: ～させてくれる means "to allow/let someone do something": 忘れさせてくれました (wasuresasete kuremashita - "made me forget" or "allowed me to forget").

🌅 朝のルーティン (Morning Routine)

田中家の朝は早いです。午前六時に目覚まし時計が鳴り、お父さんが最初に起きました。カーテンを開けると、明るい朝日が部屋に入ってきました。

Tanaka-ke no asa wa hayai desu. Gozen roku-ji ni mezamashi dokei ga nari, otousan ga saisho ni okimashita. Kaaten wo akeru to, akarui asahi ga heya ni haitte kimashita. • The Tanaka family's morning starts early. At 6 AM the alarm clock rang, and the father woke up first. When he opened the curtains, bright morning sunlight came into the room.

お母さんは台所に行って、朝ごはんの準備を始めました。ご飯を炊いて、味噌汁を作りました。焼き魚と卵焼きも用意しました。朝ごはんの良い香りが家中に広がりました。

Okaasan wa daidokoro ni itte, asagohan no junbi wo hajimemashita. Gohan wo taite, misoshiru wo tsukurimashita. Yakizakana to tamagoyaki mo youi shimashita. Asagohan no yoi kaori ga ienaka ni hirogarimashita. • The mother went to the kitchen and started preparing breakfast. She cooked rice and made miso soup. She also prepared grilled fish and rolled omelet. The good smell of breakfast spread throughout the house.
📝 Grammar Tip: ～て form (te form) connects actions in sequence: 行って (itte - "went and"), 炊いて (taite - "cooked and"). This shows multiple actions happening one after another.

七時になると、お母さんは子供たちを起こしに二階に上がりました。「起きなさい。朝ごはんができましたよ」と優しく声をかけました。長男の健太と長女のさくらがゆっくり起きてきました。

Shichi-ji ni naru to, okaasan wa kodomo-tachi wo okoshi ni nikai ni agarimashita. "Okinasai. Asagohan ga dekimashita yo" to yasashiku koe wo kakemashita. Chounan no Kenta to choujo no Sakura ga yukkuri okite kimashita. • When it became 7 o'clock, the mother went upstairs to wake the children. "Wake up. Breakfast is ready," she called out gently. The eldest son Kenta and eldest daughter Sakura slowly got up.
🎌 Cultural Note: Traditional Japanese breakfast (和朝食 wa-choushoku) typically includes rice, miso soup, grilled fish, and rolled omelet. This balanced meal is considered healthy and provides energy for the day, though many modern families also eat Western-style breakfast.

家族全員が食卓に集まりました。「いただきます」と一緒に言ってから、朝ごはんを食べ始めました。健太は学校の宿題について話し、さくらは今日の音楽の授業を楽しみにしていると話しました。

Kazoku zen'in ga shokutaku ni atsumarimashita. "Itadakimasu" to issho ni itte kara, asagohan wo tabe hajimemashita. Kenta wa gakkou no shukudai ni tsuite hanashi, Sakura wa kyou no ongaku no jugyou wo tanoshimi ni shite iru to hanashimashita. • The whole family gathered at the dining table. After saying "Itadakimasu" together, they started eating breakfast. Kenta talked about his school homework, and Sakura said she was looking forward to today's music class.

朝ごはんの後、子供たちは歯を磨いて、制服に着替えました。お父さんはネクタイを締めて、スーツを着ました。お母さんは子供たちのお弁当を作って、カバンに入れました。

Asagohan no ato, kodomo-tachi wa ha wo migaite, seifuku ni kigaemashita. Otousan wa nekutai wo shimete, suutsu wo kimashita. Okaasan wa kodomo-tachi no obentou wo tsukutte, kaban ni iremashita. • After breakfast, the children brushed their teeth and changed into their uniforms. The father tightened his necktie and put on his suit. The mother made lunch boxes for the children and put them in their bags.
📝 Grammar Tip: 着る (kiru - "to wear") is for upper body clothing and 締める (shimeru - "to tighten/fasten") is used for ties and belts. Different verbs are used for different types of wearing in Japanese.

八時前に、家族全員が玄関に集まりました。靴を履いて、「行ってきます」と言いました。お母さんは「行ってらっしゃい」と言って、みんなを見送りました。また新しい一日が始まります。

Hachi-ji mae ni, kazoku zen'in ga genkan ni atsumarimashita. Kutsu wo haite, "Itte kimasu" to iimashita. Okaasan wa "Itte rasshai" to itte, minna wo miokurimashita. Mata atarashii ichinichi ga hajimarimasu. • Before 8 o'clock, the whole family gathered at the entrance. They put on their shoes and said "Itte kimasu." The mother said "Itte rasshai" and saw everyone off. Another new day begins.
🎌 Cultural Note: "行ってきます" (itte kimasu - "I'm going and coming back") and "行ってらっしゃい" (itte rasshai - "please go and come back") are standard phrases exchanged when family members leave home. These ritualized greetings reinforce family bonds and the expectation of safe return.

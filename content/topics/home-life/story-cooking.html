👩‍🍳 家族の料理時間 (Family Cooking Time)

日曜日の午後、佐藤家のお母さんと娘の愛美が一緒に夕食を作ることにしました。今日は伝統的な日本料理を作る予定です。お母さんが「今日は肉じゃがと天ぷらを作りましょう」と言いました。

Nichiyoubi no gogo, Satou-ke no okaasan to musume no Manami ga issho ni yuushoku wo tsukuru koto ni shimashita. Kyou wa dentou-teki na nihon-ryouri wo tsukuru yotei desu. Okaasan ga "Kyou wa nikujaga to tenpura wo tsukurimashou" to iimashita. • On Sunday afternoon, Mrs. Sato and her daughter Manami decided to make dinner together. Today they plan to make traditional Japanese cuisine. Mother said, "Today let's make nikujaga and tempura."
🎌 Cultural Note: 肉じゃが (nikujaga - "meat and potatoes") is a beloved Japanese home-cooked dish, often called "Japanese comfort food." It symbolizes home cooking and mother's cooking. Many Japanese people associate it with warmth and family.

まず、材料を準備しました。じゃがいも、にんじん、玉ねぎ、牛肉を用意しました。愛美は野菜の皮を剥いて、お母さんは肉を切りました。天ぷら用のエビと野菜も用意しました。

Mazu, zairyou wo junbi shimashita. Jagaimo, ninjin, tamanegi, gyuuniku wo youi shimashita. Manami wa yasai no kawa wo muite, okaasan wa niku wo kirimashita. Tenpura-you no ebi to yasai mo youi shimashita. • First, they prepared the ingredients. They got potatoes, carrots, onions, and beef ready. Manami peeled the vegetables, and mother cut the meat. They also prepared shrimp and vegetables for tempura.

お母さんは愛美に野菜の切り方を教えました。「にんじんは乱切りにして、玉ねぎは薄く切ってね」と説明しました。愛美は慎重に包丁を使って、野菜を切りました。お母さんは「上手ですね」と褒めました。

Okaasan wa Manami ni yasai no kiri-kata wo oshiemashita. "Ninjin wa ran-giri ni shite, tamanegi wa usuku kitte ne" to setsumei shimashita. Manami wa shinchou ni houchou wo tsukatte, yasai wo kirimashita. Okaasan wa "Jouzu desu ne" to homemashita. • Mother taught Manami how to cut vegetables. "Cut the carrots into irregular chunks, and slice the onions thinly," she explained. Manami carefully used the knife and cut the vegetables. Mother praised her saying, "That's skillful."
📝 Grammar Tip: ～方 (kata) creates a noun meaning "way of doing": 切り方 (kiri-kata - "way of cutting"), 作り方 (tsukuri-kata - "way of making"), 食べ方 (tabe-kata - "way of eating").

鍋に油を入れて、肉を炒めました。肉の色が変わったら、野菜を加えました。砂糖、醤油、みりんで味付けをして、水を入れました。蓋をして、野菜が柔らかくなるまで煮込みました。

Nabe ni abura wo irete, niku wo itamemashita. Niku no iro ga kawattara, yasai wo kuwaemashita. Satou, shouyu, mirin de aji-tsuke wo shite, mizu wo iremashita. Futa wo shite, yasai ga yawarakaku naru made nikomi mashita. • They put oil in the pot and stir-fried the meat. When the meat color changed, they added vegetables. They seasoned it with sugar, soy sauce, and mirin, and added water. They covered it with a lid and simmered until the vegetables became soft.

肉じゃがが煮込んでいる間に、天ぷらの準備をしました。お母さんが天ぷら粉を水で溶いて、衣を作りました。愛美はエビと野菜に軽く粉をまぶしました。油の温度が適切になったら、一つずつ揚げ始めました。

Nikujaga ga nikonde iru aida ni, tenpura no junbi wo shimashita. Okaasan ga tenpura-ko wo mizu de toite, koromo wo tsukurimashita. Manami wa ebi to yasai ni karuku kona wo mabushimashita. Abura no ondo ga tekisetsu ni nattara, hitotsu-zutsu age hajimemashita. • While the nikujaga was simmering, they prepared the tempura. Mother dissolved tempura flour in water to make the batter. Manami lightly dusted the shrimp and vegetables with flour. When the oil temperature became appropriate, they began frying them one by one.
📝 Grammar Tip: ～ている間に (te iru aida ni) means "while something is happening": 煮込んでいる間に (nikonde iru aida ni - "while it's simmering"). This shows two actions happening in the same time period.

夕方、料理が全部できあがりました。食卓に肉じゃが、天ぷら、ご飯、味噌汁を並べました。家族全員が集まって、「いただきます」と言いました。お父さんと息子は「美味しい!」と喜びました。愛美は料理を作る楽しさを学びました。

Yuugata, ryouri ga zenbu dekiagarimashita. Shokutaku ni nikujaga, tenpura, gohan, misoshiru wo narabemashita. Kazoku zen'in ga atsumatte, "Itadakimasu" to iimashita. Otousan to musuko wa "Oishii!" to yorokobimashita. Manami wa ryouri wo tsukuru tanoshisa wo manabimashita. • In the evening, all the dishes were completed. They arranged nikujaga, tempura, rice, and miso soup on the dining table. The whole family gathered and said "Itadakimasu." Father and son rejoiced saying "Delicious!" Manami learned the joy of cooking.
🎌 Cultural Note: Teaching children to cook is an important part of Japanese family life. It passes down traditional recipes and cooking techniques through generations, and is seen as essential life education. Mother-daughter cooking time strengthens family bonds.

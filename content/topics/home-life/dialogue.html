💡 Grammar Tip: Japanese uses different verbs for "to be": います (imasu) for people/animals, あります (arimasu) for objects. Present tense verbs end in ます (masu) in polite form.
あなた
「お母さん、どこにいますか。」
Okaasan, doko ni imasu ka. • Mom, where are you?
母
「台所にいるわよ。今、夕飯の準備をしているの。」
Daidokoro ni iru wa yo. Ima, yuuhan no junbi wo shite iru no. • I'm in the kitchen. I'm preparing dinner now.
あなた
「手伝いましょうか。」
Tetsudaimashou ka. • Shall I help?
母
「ありがとう。じゃあ、テーブルを拭いてくれる？」
Arigatou. Jaa, teeburu wo fuite kureru? • Thank you. Then, could you wipe the table?
あなた
「はい、わかりました。お皿も並べますか。」
Hai, wakarimashita. Osara mo narabemasu ka. • Yes, I understand. Should I set out the plates too?
母
「お願いね。あと十分で食べられるわ。」
Onegai ne. Ato juppun de taberareru wa. • Please do. We can eat in ten minutes.
あなた
「お父さんを呼びますね。」
Otousan wo yobimasu ne. • I'll call Dad.
🎌 Cultural Note: Offering to help with household tasks (手伝いましょうか tetsudaimashou ka) is common in Japanese families. Family members often work together during meal preparation. The phrases いただきます (before eating) and ごちそうさまでした (after eating) express gratitude for the meal and are essential Japanese table manners taught from childhood.
💡 Grammar Tip: Past tense in Japanese: ました (mashita) for polite verbs, た (ta) for plain form. Example: 食べます (tabemasu - eat) → 食べました (tabemashita - ate).
あなた
「ただいま！」
Tadaima! • I'm home!
父
「おかえり。今日は遅かったね。どうしたの？」
Okaeri. Kyou wa osokatta ne. Doushita no? • Welcome home. You were late today. What happened?
あなた
「友達と買い物に行きました。それから、カフェでお茶を飲みました。」
Tomodachi to kaimono ni ikimashita. Sorekara, kafe de ocha wo nomimashita. • I went shopping with a friend. Then, we had tea at a cafe.
父
「そう。楽しかった？晩ごはんはもう食べた？」
Sou. Tanoshikatta? Ban gohan wa mou tabeta? • I see. Did you have fun? Have you eaten dinner yet?
あなた
「いいえ、まだです。お腹が空きました。」
Iie, mada desu. Onaka ga sukimashita. • No, not yet. I'm hungry.
父
「お母さんがカレーを作ったよ。冷蔵庫に入っているから、温めて食べてね。」
Okaasan ga karee wo tsukutta yo. Reizouko ni haitte iru kara, atatamete tabete ne. • Mom made curry. It's in the fridge, so heat it up and eat it.
あなた
「ありがとうございます。食べた後、お皿を洗います。」
Arigatou gozaimasu. Tabeta ato, osara wo araimasu. • Thank you very much. After I eat, I'll wash the dishes.
🎌 Cultural Note: The exchange ただいま (tadaima - "I'm home") and おかえり (okaeri - "welcome home") is a fundamental ritual in Japanese households. It's considered rude not to announce your arrival. Leftovers are common in Japanese homes, and family members often heat up food left in the refrigerator. Offering to wash dishes shows consideration for family members.
💡 Grammar Tip: Future tense uses the same form as present tense in Japanese. Context and time markers (明日 ashita - tomorrow, 来週 raishuu - next week) indicate future actions.
あなた
「お母さん、明日の予定は？」
Okaasan, ashita no yotei wa? • Mom, what's the plan for tomorrow?
母
「午前中に掃除をします。午後はスーパーに買い物に行くつもりです。」
Gozenchuu ni souji wo shimasu. Gogo wa suupaa ni kaimono ni iku tsumori desu. • I'll clean in the morning. In the afternoon, I plan to go shopping at the supermarket.
あなた
「僕も手伝います。一緒に掃除しましょう。」
Boku mo tetsudaimasu. Issho ni souji shimashou. • I'll help too. Let's clean together.
母
「ありがとう。じゃあ、あなたは自分の部屋とリビングルームを掃除してくれる？」
Arigatou. Jaa, anata wa jibun no heya to ribingu ruumu wo souji shite kureru? • Thank you. Then, could you clean your own room and the living room?
あなた
「はい、わかりました。洗濯もしますか。」
Hai, wakarimashita. Sentaku mo shimasu ka. • Yes, I understand. Should I do laundry too?
母
「お願いね。洗濯が終わったら、干してくださいね。」
Onegai ne. Sentaku ga owattara, hoshite kudasai ne. • Please do. When the laundry is done, please hang it to dry.
あなた
「もちろん。午後は一緒に買い物に行きます。」
Mochiron. Gogo wa issho ni kaimono ni ikimasu. • Of course. I'll go shopping with you in the afternoon.
🎌 Cultural Note: In Japanese households, hanging laundry to dry (干す hosu) is still very common, even in modern apartments. Many homes have a designated area on the balcony or veranda for drying clothes. This practice is both energy-efficient and believed to give clothes a fresher smell. Family members often coordinate household tasks on weekends.
💡 Grammar Tip: Advanced conversation uses conditional forms (たら tara - if/when), causative forms (させる saseru - to make someone do), and honorific language (お〜になる o~ni naru).
母
「来週、おばあちゃんが泊まりに来るの。家を綺麗にしておかないとね。」
Raishuu, obaachan ga tomari ni kuru no. Ie wo kirei ni shite okanai to ne. • Grandma is coming to stay next week. We need to keep the house clean.
あなた
「そうですか。客間を準備しないといけませんね。布団を干したり、部屋を掃除したりしましょう。」
Sou desu ka. Kyakuma wo junbi shinai to ikemasen ne. Futon wo hoshitari, heya wo souji shitari shimashou. • I see. We need to prepare the guest room. Let's air out the futon and clean the room.
父
「私は庭の手入れをしておきます。おばあちゃんは庭を見るのが好きだから、花を植えておこうと思います。」
Watashi wa niwa no teire wo shite okimasu. Obaachan wa niwa wo miru no ga suki dakara, hana wo uete okou to omoimasu. • I'll take care of the garden. Grandma likes looking at the garden, so I'm thinking of planting flowers.
母
「それはいい考えね。おばあちゃんが来られたら、好きな和食を作りたいと思っているの。何がいいかしら。」
Sore wa ii kangae ne. Obaachan ga koraretara, suki na washoku wo tsukuritai to omotte iru no. Nani ga ii kashira. • That's a good idea. When Grandma comes, I want to make her favorite Japanese food. What would be good?
あなた
「おばあちゃんは煮物が大好きですよね。あと、お味噌汁も毎日飲みたがっていらっしゃいました。」
Obaachan wa nimono ga daisuki desu yo ne. Ato, omisoshiru mo mainichi nomitagatte irasshaimashita. • Grandma loves simmered dishes. Also, she always wanted to have miso soup every day.
母
「そうね。じゃあ、筑前煮を作りましょう。お味噌汁の具も色々用意しておかないとね。」
Sou ne. Jaa, chikuzen-ni wo tsukurimashou. Omisoshiru no gu mo iroiro youi shite okanai to ne. • That's right. Then, let's make chikuzen-ni. We need to prepare various ingredients for miso soup too.
父
「家族みんなで協力すれば、おばあちゃんに喜んでもらえるはずです。楽しみですね。」
Kazoku minna de kyouryoku sureba, obaachan ni yorokonde moraeru hazu desu. Tanoshimi desu ne. • If we all work together as a family, we should be able to make Grandma happy. I'm looking forward to it.
🎌 Cultural Note: Multi-generational living and frequent family visits are common in Japan. Preparing the 客間 (kyakuma - guest room) with careful attention shows respect for elderly family members. Traditional Japanese hospitality extends to family, with members going to great lengths to ensure comfort. Dishes like 筑前煮 (chikuzen-ni - a Kyushu-style simmered dish) and homemade 味噌汁 (misoshiru) represent the warmth of home cooking and family bonds.

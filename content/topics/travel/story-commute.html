🚇 都市通勤 (City Commute)

佐藤さんは毎朝六時に起きて、七時の電車で会社に通勤しています。自宅から最寄りの駅まで徒歩十分、そこから電車で三十分かかります。

Satou-san wa maiasa roku-ji ni okite, shichi-ji no densha de kaisha ni tsuukin shite imasu. Jitaku kara moyori no eki made toho juppun, soko kara densha de sanjuppun kakarimasu. • Mr. Sato gets up at 6 AM every morning and commutes to work on the 7 o'clock train. It's a 10-minute walk from his home to the nearest station, and from there it takes 30 minutes by train.
🎌 Cultural Note: Japanese commuter trains during rush hour (ラッシュアワー rasshu awaa or 通勤ラッシュ tsuukin rasshu) are famously crowded. In major cities like Tokyo, trains can reach 200% capacity during peak hours. Station staff sometimes help push passengers into trains to ensure doors can close.

駅に着くと、改札口でICカードをタッチして入ります。佐藤さんはSuicaカードを使っています。ホームは既に人でいっぱいで、電車を待つ列ができていました。

Eki ni tsuku to, kaisatsu-guchi de IC kaado wo tacchi shite hairimasu. Satou-san wa Suica kaado wo tsukatte imasu. Hoomu wa sude ni hito de ippai de, densha wo matsu retsu ga dekite imashita. • When he arrives at the station, he touches his IC card at the ticket gate and enters. Mr. Sato uses a Suica card. The platform was already full of people, with lines forming to wait for the train.
📝 Grammar Tip: 既に (sude ni) means "already" and is more formal than もう (mou). It's commonly used in news, announcements, and polite conversation.

電車が到着すると、乗客が降りるのを待ってから乗り込みます。朝のラッシュアワーなので、車内は非常に混雑していました。佐藤さんは吊り革につかまり、スマートフォンでニュースを読みました。

Densha ga touchaku suru to, joukyaku ga oriru no wo matte kara norikomi masu. Asa no rasshu awaa na node, shanai wa hijou ni konzatsu shite imashita. Satou-san wa tsurikawa ni tsukamari, sumaato-fon de nyuusu wo yomimashita. • When the train arrives, passengers wait for others to get off before boarding. Because it's morning rush hour, the inside of the car was extremely crowded. Mr. Sato held onto a strap handle and read the news on his smartphone.

途中で地下鉄に乗り換えます。乗り換えの通路は長くて複雑ですが、佐藤さんは毎日同じルートなので慣れています。案内表示に従って歩き、次の電車に乗りました。

Tochuu de chikatetsu ni norikae masu. Norikae no tsuuro wa nagakute fukuzatsu desu ga, Satou-san wa mainichi onaji ruuto na node narete imasu. Annai-hyouji ni shitagatte aruki, tsugi no densha ni norimashita. • Halfway through, he transfers to the subway. The transfer passageway is long and complex, but Mr. Sato is used to it because he takes the same route every day. He walked following the directional signs and boarded the next train.
🎌 Cultural Note: Major Tokyo stations like Shinjuku and Shibuya have incredibly complex layouts with multiple train lines, exits, and underground shopping areas. 乗り換え (norikae - transfers) can involve walking 10-15 minutes through underground passages. Clear signage (案内表示 annai-hyouji) is essential for navigation.

会社の最寄り駅に到着し、改札を出ました。駅前のコンビニで朝食のおにぎりとコーヒーを買い、オフィスビルに向かいました。エレベーターで十二階まで上がり、定刻通りに到着しました。

Kaisha no moyori eki ni touchaku shi, kaisatsu wo demashita. Ekimae no konbini de choushoku no onigiri to koohii wo kai, ofisu-biru ni mukaimashita. Erebeetaa de juuni-kai made agari, teikoku-doori ni touchaku shimashita. • He arrived at the nearest station to his company and exited the ticket gate. He bought rice balls and coffee for breakfast at a convenience store in front of the station and headed to the office building. He went up to the 12th floor by elevator and arrived on time.
📝 Grammar Tip: ～通り (doori) after a noun means "as scheduled/expected": 定刻通り (teikoku-doori - "on time"), 予定通り (yotei-doori - "as planned"), 期待通り (kitai-doori - "as expected").

帰りの電車も混んでいますが、朝ほどではありません。佐藤さんは座席に座ることができ、疲れた一日を振り返りながら帰宅しました。この通勤は大変ですが、もう生活の一部になっています。

Kaeri no densha mo konde imasu ga, asa hodo dewa arimasen. Satou-san wa zaseki ni suwaru koto ga deki, tsukareta ichinichi wo furikaeri nagara kitaku shimashita. Kono tsuukin wa taihen desu ga, mou seikatsu no ichibu ni natte imasu. • The evening train is also crowded, but not as much as the morning. Mr. Sato was able to sit in a seat and went home while reflecting on his tiring day. This commute is difficult, but it has already become part of his life.
📝 Grammar Tip: ～ながら (nagara) means "while doing": 振り返りながら (furikaeri nagara - "while reflecting"), 歩きながら (aruki nagara - "while walking"). It connects two simultaneous actions performed by the same person.
